# Experience Reports

Use for reviewing past work, exchanging practices, and describing cases. Follow the current audience and purpose.

## Structure

- Start from the user's topic and identify the problem, actual actions, mechanism, case or data, and observed results.
- Group related actions into distinct practices. Each point needs a concrete source. Use fewer points when evidence is thin.
- Give space to the main details and preserve requested cases and their meaning. Do not allocate equal space mechanically across attachments.
- Use precise titles and structural distinctions. End with supported experience, limitations, or next steps appropriate to the audience.

## Evidence

Separate actions from effects. Training counts do not establish satisfaction. A new mechanism does not establish maturity. A case does not establish widespread adoption. Comparisons, rankings, awards, and claims of burden reduction require evidence.

Keep each figure with its organization, period, definition, unit, and qualifiers. Past samples cannot supply current outcomes. If no factual work material exists, ask for the smallest concrete example. If requested, provide an outline or working draft with clearly open sections.

Before delivery, check that the user's emphasis is visible, each practice has support, and editing has preserved examples and the strength of the evidence.
